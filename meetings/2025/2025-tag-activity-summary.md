## February 4 — 2025 Planning and Vocabulary Foundations

The year kicked off with an East/West split meeting (the new dual-timezone format) focused on setting priorities. Key discussion items included a proposal to use Wikidata as a broker for canonical geographic region definitions (specifically in the context of WGSRPD), draft metadata schemes for controlled vocabularies and classification systems, a new TDWG Glossary initiative, and updates to StaDocGen. Kit Lewers introduced remote sensing as a potential new area of collaboration, with interest in aligning with organizations like NASA, ESA, and NEON on standards and controlled vocabularies.

**Attendees:** Ben Norton, Kit Lewers, Cecilie Svenningsen, John Wieczorek, David Fichtmueller, Steve Baskauf, John Kunze, Erica Krimmel, David Shorthouse, Stan Blum, Greg Whitbread

## June 10 — Controlled Vocabulary Services

John Kunze presented YAMZ (https://yamz.net/), a "simplicity by design" vocabulary service built around a master glossary approach from which individual vocabularies can be constructed. The session was a focused technical walkthrough of the system's design and key features.

**Attendees:** John Wieczorek, John Kunze, Kate Webbink, Sam Leeflang, Cecilie Svenningsen, Greg Whitbread, Ben Norton

## September 30 — Australian Government Vocabulary Collaboration

The most significant meeting of the year, run as East/West sessions. Nicholas Car (chair of the Linked Data Working Group) presented technical infrastructure for vocabulary publishing using knowledge graphs and the **Prez** tool, drawing on Australian government biodiversity vocabulary systems. The group discussed a formal collaboration between TDWG and the Australian government to host and manage TDWG vocabularies. The Mineralogy Extension was identified as the first test case.
